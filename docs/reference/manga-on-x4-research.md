# Manga on the Xteink X4 / X4 Pro: what others do

> Desk research, **2026-09-25**, for `../v0.2/manga.md`. Sources: GitHub repos, issues and PRs, tool
> READMEs, blog posts and reviews, linked inline. Reddit, Discord and video content could only be seen
> through search summaries, so first-hand user reports are thin. No source measured furigana
> legibility, screentone rendering or 1-bit vs 2-bit side by side. Some of the "Implications" in §6
> were since settled by the spike (`../v0.2/manga.md` §4): PSRAM works on our fork (P0), and gutters
> alone turned out not to be enough to place cuts.

## Summary: manga reaches the X4 through converters

Manga on the Xteink X4 and X4 Pro works because of desktop and browser converters, not because of any firmware feature. Every working setup found rotates, slices and dithers pages on a computer, writes Xteink's uncompressed XTC (1-bit) or XTCH (2-bit) container, and then pages through the result on the device. The dominant tools all descend from **tazua/cbz2xtc** (December 2025). The browser port **XTC.js** (xtcjs.app, 213 stars, active September 2026) is the mainstream option, and the **srokl/cbz2xtc** fork offers the most processing controls. The usual recipe is to rotate each page 90°, cut it into two or three overlapping landscape strips (right-hand piece first for Japanese), and dither with error diffusion, using 2-bit XTCH when the reader can handle it. Every slicer in use cuts at fixed positions and adds overlap to avoid slicing through speech balloons. None of them finds panel gutters. Firmware adds little. Stock firmware has a tuned fast grayscale waveform for comic pages, and CrossPoint's XTC reader is locked to portrait with no zoom, pan or reading-direction logic. No "CrossPoint++" or manga-focused CrossPoint fork was found. For OCR, the only e-ink integration found is a small KOReader plugin that reads mokuro's line-level JSON, and no project anywhere ships precomputed word boxes. A pre-OCR'd, gutter-cut, word-boxed XTC pipeline for Lexipoint would therefore be new on two fronts, and it can reuse existing tools for most of the hard parts.

## 1. One converter family does all the manga work

No Xteink-branded manga converter turned up in searches ([xtctool.com search results](https://xtctool.com/)). The lineage starts with **tazua/cbz2xtc**, a Python CLI. By default it splits each page in half, **rotates 90°**, pads to 480×800 and applies Floyd-Steinberg dithering. It outputs 1-bit only, and it has been dormant since January 2026 ([tazua README](https://github.com/tazua/cbz2xtc/blob/main/README.md)). **XTC.js** began as its TypeScript port and now runs fully in the browser. It accepts CBZ/CBR, PDF, images and even video. It has presets for X4/X4 Pro (480×800) and X3 (528×792), five dither modes, a contrast slider, "overlapping thirds" or halves splitting, and a right-to-left wide-page splitter ([varo6/xtcjs](https://github.com/varo6/xtcjs)). The sources disagree on whether the main XTC.js app exports 2-bit XTCH. Its README is ambiguous, and a directory listing credits a separate fork with adding "2-bit Greyscale and Manhwa Portrait Strip" ([readme.club resources](https://www.readme.club/resources)).

Power users use **srokl/cbz2xtc**. It adds `--2bit` XTCH output and nine dither algorithms (Stucki default, Zhou-Fang "recommended for e-ink", Atkinson, Ostromoukhov, stochastic and others). Other options include `--gamma`, `--contrast-boost` and percentage margin crop, a three-piece `--landscape-page-split rtl|ltr`, horizontal and vertical split grids with configurable overlap (**70% horizontal, 5% vertical by default**), a `--manhwa` long-strip mode, and "overview" pages that show the full page before its slices. Its suggested Japanese manga command is `--2bit --landscape-page-split rtl --sideways-overviews --downscale bicubic` ([srokl/cbz2xtc](https://github.com/srokl/cbz2xtc)). A blog walkthrough recommends almost exactly this setup with Zhou-Fang dithering and calls the result "surprisingly enjoyable" ([www-gem](https://www-gem.codeberg.page/cli_x4_manga/)).

Smaller tools fill niches. **XteinkImageRefiner** is a Japanese GUI whose pipeline runs margin trim, auto-rotate, blur, resize, sharpen, CLAHE and contrast before dithering. It exports XTC, XTCH, EPUB3 or CBZ, and its author found "contrast adjustment with XTCH" the most effective way to make pages legible ([note.com](https://note.com/feeeen/n/n9b036c73da6f?hl=en)). **xtctool** handles PDF and Typst with supersampling and adjustable 4-level thresholds ([chazeon/xtctool](https://github.com/chazeon/xtctool)). **imgs-to-xtc** scrapes web images into landscape segments ([jakebirkes/imgs-to-xtc](https://github.com/jakebirkes/imgs-to-xtc)). **KCC has no Xteink profile** and cannot write XTC. Its crop, gamma and Panel View features target Kindle ([ciromattia/kcc](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc)), and no documented pipeline uses KCC as a preprocessing step for Xteink.

The format itself is simple. An XTC/XTCH file has a 56-byte header, an optional 256-byte metadata block, a 16-byte page index entry per page, optional 96-byte chapter entries, and page bitmaps that each carry a 22-byte header. XTG pages are 1 bpp and row-major. XTH pages are two bit planes stored **column-major, with columns scanned right-to-left**, and the value mapping is non-monotonic (01 = dark grey, 10 = light grey). A header byte, `readDirection`, encodes L→R, R→L or top-to-bottom. The format defines a compression flag, but **no reader implements compression** ([CrazyCoder spec gist](https://gist.github.com/CrazyCoder/b125f26d6987c0620058249f59f1327d)). Without compression, file size depends only on page count: about 48 KB per 1-bit page and 96 KB per 2-bit page at 480×800. By that arithmetic, a 200-page volume cut into thirds comes to roughly 29 MB as XTC or 58 MB as XTCH (calculated, not measured). srokl's LZ4 `.xtcz` saves about 30%, but CrossPoint does not read it ([CrossPoint #2573](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/issues/2573)).

## 2. Sideways strips cut blind, with overlap as the only safeguard

The community agrees that a full portrait page at 480×800 on a 4.3" screen is too small for dialogue. One reviewer files "manga with small text" under "weak experience" ([MobilesTalk](https://mobilestalk.net/xteink-x4-review-after-three-months-i-love-this-teeny-tiny-e-reader-more-than-ever/)), and CrossPoint's maintainers declined on-device CBZ for the same readability reason ([Discussion #2015](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/discussions/2015)). The fix is magnification by slicing. A page cut into horizontal bands and rotated 90° fills the 800-pixel axis, and the reader holds the device sideways. XTC.js PR #46 gives the clearest evidence: squeezed double-page spreads had left dialogue unreadable, and after RTL wide-page splitting was added, **page count rose from 51 to 61 and "the affected dialogue is now readable"** on real X4 hardware ([xtcjs PR #46](https://github.com/varo6/xtcjs/pull/46)).

Every cut is geometric. XTC.js says so directly ("geometric splitting, not panel detection"), and srokl's README has no panel option ([varo6/xtcjs](https://github.com/varo6/xtcjs); [srokl/cbz2xtc](https://github.com/srokl/cbz2xtc)). One blog says cbz2xtc detects panels with OpenCV or YOLO ([www-gem](https://www-gem.codeberg.page/cli_x4_manga/)), but the current READMEs contradict it, so treat that claim as unverified. Because the tools cannot see where balloons are, they rely on heavy overlap (up to 70%) and optional overview pages. The price is more pages, repeated content and a larger file. Panel detection does exist elsewhere: kumiko uses OpenCV contours ([njean42/kumiko](https://github.com/njean42/kumiko)), and panelizer combines computer vision, a YOLO/SAM fallback and optional VLM ordering at about 80% RTL-order accuracy. Panelizer is now archived, and it documents failures on borderless panels, art that bleeds into gutters, and irregular layouts ([hummat/panelizer](https://github.com/hummat/panelizer)). No report was found of either tool being used on an X4. Right-to-left order is handled by the order of the emitted slices: the right piece comes first, the cover stays whole, and panoramic art can opt out. Users accept that the tools "won't perfectly handle every double-page spread" ([www-gem](https://www-gem.codeberg.page/cli_x4_manga/)).

The closest analogue to the Lexipoint idea is **matcha-reader**, a CrossPoint fork "focused on reading Japanese books & manga with built-in learning tools". It ships a converter to its own **panel-by-panel format** rather than XTC ([GitHub topic: xteink](https://github.com/topics/xteink?l=c); [readme.club](https://www.readme.club/resources)). Its internals were not examined, and they deserve a direct look.

## 3. Two-bit grayscale looks best but costs memory, speed and ghosting

Tool authors, not independent testers, supply the dithering advice, and it largely agrees. XTC.js recommends Floyd-Steinberg with Medium contrast for manga art and Atkinson for text-heavy pages ([varo6/xtcjs](https://github.com/varo6/xtcjs)). tazua finds ordered dithering "often clearer for dense text" ([tazua README](https://github.com/tazua/cbz2xtc/blob/main/README.md)). srokl calls 2-bit the "Highest overall quality" and undithered output best for text-only pages ([srokl/cbz2xtc](https://github.com/srokl/cbz2xtc)). No source tests screentone moiré or furigana legibility. The logical inference is that error diffusion on 1-bit re-dithers screentone into noise, and that is what drives people toward XTCH and area-based downscaling. Nobody has published side-by-side captures, though.

The device side complicates the choice. An XTCH page needs a **contiguous 96,000-byte buffer**. On ESP32-C3 X4s, heap fragmentation repeatedly produced "Memory error" ([#814](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/issues/814)), and open PRs stream 1-bit rows instead ([#2287](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/pull/2287)). The X4 Pro's 8 MB PSRAM should make the buffer trivial. However, a September 2026 CrossPoint RC left **PSRAM uninitialized on the Pro**, so a 572-page XTCH failed while the same book as XTC worked ([#3330](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/issues/3330)). The breakage was traced to a platform-espressif32 version change ([#3429](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/issues/3429)). Refresh quality is the other cost. Stock Chinese firmware 3.1.4+ added a fast grayscale LUT that a community member labelled "Comic reading - fast page turns". When ported to CrossPoint it matched stock speed, while CrossPoint's own 2-bit path is slower. An X4 Pro user with the UC8279 panel also reported grayscale text looking more jagged than on the original X4 ([#215](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/issues/215)). A factory-LUT PR is still open ([#1614](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/pull/1614)). Reddit users reportedly see more ghosting with XTCH than with XTC. That report comes second-hand through a search summary ([Discussion #2932](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/discussions/2932)).

## 4. Firmware reads pre-rendered pages and does little else

The official X4 Pro FAQ lists only EPUB, TXT, JPG and BMP, and it does not mention comics, XTC or zoom ([Xteink FAQ](https://www.xteink.com/blogs/product/x4-pro-faq-specs-support)). A retail listing claims xtc, xtch and xtcz support ([Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/XTEINK-Pocket-Ink-Book-Reader/dp/B0GR4BCJK3)). The conflict is unresolved. No evidence was found of stock zoom, pan, panel view or on-device RTL settings. RTL and landscape are baked into the file by the converter.

Upstream **CrossPoint** (v1.6.0, September 2026) reads EPUB, XTC/XTCH, TXT and BMP ([README](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/blob/develop/README.md)). Its `XtcReaderActivity` **forces portrait orientation**. It offers page turns, long-press skip and chapter selection when the file has a table of contents. It has **no zoom, pan or reading-direction code**, and page order always follows the file ([XtcReaderActivity.cpp](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/blob/develop/src/activities/reader/XtcReaderActivity.cpp)). SCOPE.md rules PDF and fixed-layout rendering out of scope because of "constant panning and zooming" on e-ink, and it treats the ESP32-C3 as the design ceiling ([SCOPE.md](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/blob/develop/SCOPE.md)). Manga-adjacent requests are still open: go-to-page and percent jumps for XTC ([#1004](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/issues/1004)), auto page turn ([#2545](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/issues/2545)), an XTC reader menu ([PR #1872](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/pull/1872)) and zoom ([Discussion #3485](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/discussions/3485)). A manga that loses its TOC during conversion gets no chapter navigation, and the maintainers attribute that to the converter ([#753](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-reader/issues/753)).

**No fork called "CrossPoint++" exists** on GitHub or the open web as of this research. It may be a Reddit nickname or a private build. The forks listed in the CrossPoint README (CrossInk, papyrix, inx, Witch(hunt)) are text-first. CrossInk warns that comics "may load slowly or fail under memory pressure" ([CrossInk](https://github.com/uxjulia/CrossInk)). papyrix adds XTC/XTCH, four orientations and a separate ESP32-S3 build ([papyrix-reader](https://github.com/bigbag/papyrix-reader)). Aside from matcha-reader, no fork advertises CBZ reading, panel view or strip scrolling.

## 5. Manga OCR on e-ink stops at the speech bubble

**mokuro** (v0.2.5, July 2026) is still the standard PC pipeline for learners. It runs comic-text-detector followed by manga-ocr and writes one `.mokuro` JSON per volume ([PyPI mokuro](https://pypi.org/project/mokuro/)). Each page stores `img_width`/`img_height` and a list of blocks. A block holds an axis-aligned `box`, a `vertical` flag, a `font_size` estimate, and parallel `lines` / `lines_coords` arrays of line polygons. **Coordinates stop at the line level, with no per-character or per-word boxes** ([manga_page_ocr.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kha-white/mokuro/master/mokuro/manga_page_ocr.py)). Browser readers get character hit-testing for free by laying the text out as an HTML overlay. The ESP32 gets nothing comparable. manga-ocr (about 400 MB, Apache-2.0) handles vertical text and furigana. Its known weaknesses are hallucinating text over art, handwriting, and long passages ([manga-ocr](https://github.com/kha-white/manga-ocr)). For higher accuracy, PaddleOCR-VL-For-Manga reaches **70% full-sentence accuracy on Manga109-s crops, against 27% for its base model**. Its main errors are full-width versus half-width punctuation and digits ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/jzhang533/PaddleOCR-VL-For-Manga)). faster-mokuro adds Apple Silicon MPS support and batched inference, and can bundle the .mokuro inside the CBZ, though it publishes no benchmarks ([faster-mokuro](https://github.com/JanLancelot/faster-mokuro)). No learner-oriented "mokuro for Chinese" exists. Detection is language-agnostic, but OCR would need PaddleOCR or a VLM, and segmentation would need jieba or pkuseg.

The only e-ink integration found is **mokuroreader-koreader** (25 stars). It reads a CBZ with an embedded .mokuro. The user taps a bubble to open its OCR text in a popup, then drags across characters to send the selection to KOReader's dictionary ([mokuroreader-koreader](https://github.com/Magyarapointe/mokuroreader-koreader)). Word boundaries come from the user's finger plus KOReader's Yomichan-style longest-match deinflector, not from a morphological analyzer ([koplugin.japanese](https://koreader.rocks/doc/modules/koplugin.japanese.html); [KOReader #11728](https://github.com/koreader/koreader/issues/11728)). On Kindle, Mokuro2Pdf bakes an invisible text layer into a PDF instead ([Mokuro2Pdf](https://github.com/Kartoffel0/Mokuro2Pdf)). The design pattern on e-ink is consistent: pick a region first, then choose the word from text rather than from pixels. No project precomputes word boxes with lemmas.

## 6. Implications for Lexipoint's pre-OCR'd manga design

**Store the sidecar in the coordinate space of the final XTC page, not the source page.** The CrossPoint XTC reader draws each stored 480×800 page as-is in portrait. A landscape strip is therefore a rotated image, and the touch panel reports portrait coordinates. If the Mac pipeline applies the same crop, scale and 90° rotation to mokuro's line quads that it applies to the pixels, and keys the output by XTC page index, then hit-testing on the device becomes a lookup of pre-transformed rectangles. The ESP32 then never needs the source geometry. The less obvious consequence is the popup card. The reader holds the device sideways, but the reader activity is locked to portrait, so the card must be drawn rotated to match the strip or it will appear sideways. Budget for a landscape render path for the card on strip pages. Side-button word stepping also has to follow the rotated reading order: top band first, then right-to-left within the band for Japanese.

**Gutter-snapped cuts are Lexipoint's clearest differentiator, and they also simplify the sidecar.** Existing tools overlap strips because they cut blind. A cutter that searches the row-darkness profile near each target third for a white horizontal band, and falls back to overlap only when no gutter exists (bleed art, borderless panels), would produce fewer pages and keep every balloon whole. With balloons intact, each word box belongs to exactly one strip. Mokuro's `box` gives a second signal: never place a cut through a text block. Keep an optional whole-page overview, since users clearly value page context.

**Do word segmentation on the server and interpolate characters from line quads.** mokuro gives only line polygons, so split each line evenly along its reading axis, group characters into words with the Lexirise tokenizer, and pad the resulting boxes. OCR insertions, dropped characters and leaked furigana will make interpolated positions drift, so resolve a tap to the nearest word within the tapped block rather than requiring an exact hit. On a 480-pixel axis a word can be a few pixels wide, so tap-to-block followed by side-button stepping is the right default. That matches the approved card behaviour and the KOReader precedent. Store lemmas or entry IDs in the sidecar so the device never needs to deinflect. Normalize full-width and half-width punctuation after OCR. Keep a path open to re-OCR mokuro's line crops with PaddleOCR-VL-For-Manga where accuracy matters. For Chinese, swap in a Chinese OCR model and expect horizontal, often colour or webtoon-format pages.

**Default to 2-bit XTCH on the X4 Pro and pin the PSRAM fix.** The Pro's PSRAM removes the 96 KB allocation risk that dominates C3 devices, but only once #3330/#3429 are resolved in the fork's platform pin. Test XTCH on the actual UC8279 panel, because grayscale quality there is disputed. Consider carrying the factory fast LUT from #215/#1614 to speed up page turns. Write the chapter table and metadata, since CrossPoint's chapter jump depends on them. Keep the files as plain XTC/XTCH rather than `.xtcz`. Plan storage around the uncompressed arithmetic: about 96 KB per strip means tens of MB per volume, while a word-box sidecar adds only kilobytes.

## 7. Conclusion

The X4 manga scene has settled on a split of labour: the device only displays pages, and all the intelligence runs on a PC. That split favours Lexipoint's plan, because the sidecar can live in the same place as the slicing. The weakest link today is the cut itself, not dithering or firmware. Every tool pays for blind geometric cuts with overlap, duplicate pages and broken balloons, and a text-aware, gutter-snapping cutter fixes that while also producing clean one-strip-per-word geometry for lookup. Nobody has shipped word-level boxes for manga on any e-ink device, including KOReader. Lexipoint would therefore be first to offer one-tap lookup on manga images, not just a CrossPoint port of an existing feature. The open risks are specific and testable: X4 Pro PSRAM initialization, grayscale quality on the UC8279 panel, drift in interpolated character boxes, and drawing the card in landscape inside a portrait-locked reader. matcha-reader's panel format is the one piece of prior art that should be inspected before building.
